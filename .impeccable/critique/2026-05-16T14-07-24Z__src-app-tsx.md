## Anti-Patterns Verdict

This does not read as a generic AI SaaS shell because the puzzle board, target badges, difficulty systems, and generated-level mechanics are specific. It does have AI-ish surface habits: blurred glow blobs, glassy oversized panels, many rounded pill chips, and a crowded status strip.

The deterministic scan found 4 warnings, all `pure-black-white` background usage in `src/App.tsx`: line 1213 progress track, line 1247 lost overlay, line 1279 unlock overlay, line 1302 win overlay. These are low-risk but easy to polish by tinting toward the app background instead of `bg-black/*`.

Browser visual overlays were skipped because this session does not have a callable browser automation module installed. The CLI detector was run against `src`.

## Overall Impression

The product has a real identity: compact, tactile, and puzzle-first. The biggest opportunity is to protect the cozy daily ritual from systems noise. Right now the player sees the board plus difficulty, stars, chips, modifier stack, missions, and chapter progress at once.

## What's Working

- The core board has strong affordance: cells, row/column targets, selected/erased states, hearts, and correct count are all legible game objects.
- Progression is meaningful without being purely casino-like: chapters, levels, missions, and unlock progress give return motivation.
- The visual tone is warmer than a sterile math tool, especially with rose accents, soft panels, haptics, and light/dark support.

## Priority Issues

**[P1] The first screen exposes too many systems at once**
Why it matters: casual-break players need immediate puzzle focus; the current screen competes for attention with difficulty cards, status chips, modifier stack, missions, and progress.
Fix: keep board, hearts, current tool, and one concise goal visible; collapse missions/progress/modifier details behind inline disclosure until relevant.
Suggested command: impeccable distill or impeccable layout.

**[P1] Core rules and modifier language need a clearer first-play path**
Why it matters: puzzle enthusiasts will infer the game, but first-timers may not understand Select vs Erase, target products, Fog, Cipher, No Echo, or Hold quickly enough.
Fix: add a compact first-run rule strip and contextual explanations at the exact point a modifier appears; rename or soften jargon where possible.
Suggested command: impeccable onboard or impeccable clarify.

**[P2] Mobile ergonomics are not yet thumb-first**
Why it matters: the stated context is quick mobile breaks. Top toolbar actions are high on the screen, and mode controls live after the board on single-column layouts, so repeated Select/Erase switching can require scrolling.
Fix: pin the active tool switcher near the board or bottom thumb zone; move secondary actions like theme and reroll into a compact menu or lower action rail.
Suggested command: impeccable adapt or impeccable layout.

**[P2] Result and failure copy is more abstract than cozy**
Why it matters: the product brief says cozy daily ritual. Lines like "reward summary is ready," "pressure as part of the puzzle," and "hidden gate opened" feel system-heavy when the player needs reassurance or delight.
Fix: make win/loss copy shorter, warmer, and more action-oriented.
Suggested command: impeccable clarify.

**[P3] Visual polish has a few AI-slop tells**
Why it matters: glow blobs, glass panels, very large radii, and pure black overlays make the surface feel more template-like than hand-tuned.
Fix: tint overlays toward app-bg, reduce background glow dominance, tighten panel radius, and reserve blur for moments that need depth.
Suggested command: impeccable quieter or impeccable polish.

## Cognitive Load

Checklist failures: 4. The main failures are single focus, minimal choices, one-thing-at-a-time sequencing, and progressive disclosure. Grouping is decent, but too many grouped systems are visible simultaneously.

## Persona Red Flags

**Casey, distracted mobile user**: Important actions are top-positioned, tool switching may sit below the board on mobile, and the dense status strip asks for more scanning than a quick break should require.

**Jordan, first-timer**: The first action is not explained in plain language. Terms like Modifier Stack, Cipher, No Echo, Blind, and Hold assume the player will experiment rather than understand.

**Sam, accessibility-dependent user**: Board cells are buttons, which is good, but cell-level aria labels and live announcements for state changes are not apparent from source. Color and tiny text labels carry too much state meaning in some cells.

**Alex, puzzle enthusiast**: The app has depth, but no visible expert accelerators such as keyboard shortcuts for Select/Erase, hint, retry, or next level.

## Minor Observations

- The Georgia heading gives personality, but product UI labels and data should stay closer to the system stack.
- The difficulty carousel has five visible options, just over the ideal decision count.
- The side panel is useful, but its repeated card structure makes all secondary systems feel equally important.

## Questions to Consider

- What if the default screen showed only the board, mode, hearts, and today’s immediate rule?
- Which systems are daily ritual, and which are progression admin?
- Should modifiers feel like named powers, or like quiet constraints the board teaches naturally?
